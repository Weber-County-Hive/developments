Pleasant Plains, in Plain Words

A plan for up to 3,500 homes in Plain City. Here is what it is, when the city decides, and how to be heard.

Plain City

Posted Oct. 26, 2026 · Updated Oct. 26, 2026

Come and be heard

Thursday, Nov. 12, 2026 · 7 p.m.
Plain City Hall, 4160 W. 2200 North

This is the public hearing. Questions about speaking or written comments: (801) 731-4908.

What is it?

A company called Cole West Land Partners wants to build on about 1,558 acres of Jackson family land in Plain City. The draft plan allows:

up to 3,500
Homes
300
Acres of shops, offices and industry

Backyard cottages (ADUs) would not count toward the 3,500.

Cole West builds homes across Utah. Its biggest project found so far is about 1,260 lots in Erda. Pleasant Plains would be almost three times that size.

In August, Cole West told the local water district it has more than 3,000 acre-feet of water rights "available for consideration." The district has not voted on anything yet.

When does the city decide?

  • Nov. 12, 7 p.m.: public hearing. Anyone can come.
  • By Nov. 23: the City Council must say yes or no. The developer used a state law to set this deadline.

Can the city say no?

Yes. State law gives the council two choices: approve or deny. Plain City's own rules say the city can reject a plan like this "for any reason." If it says no, it has to write down why.

If the city says no, the land can't simply switch to county rules. It would first have to leave the city, which takes a petition, a city hearing and possibly a court case.

What should people ask?

  • Is there enough of the right kind of water?
  • Can the sewer plant handle it, and who pays to grow it?
  • Who pays to widen the roads we already drive?
  • If some land is wetlands and can't be built on, why doesn't the number of homes go down?
  • Should the developer decide on its own how big each phase is?
  • Should the deal renew by itself every 10 years, up to 30?
  • City rules call for "extreme benefits" to the city. What are they?

What can I do?

  • Go to the hearing on Nov. 12 at 7 p.m. Bring a neighbor.
  • Read the city's notice.
  • Ask the council to post the final version of the agreement before it votes.
  • See other projects on the developments map.
