Altura, in Plain Words

A big housing plan for the hills above North Ogden. Here is what it is and where it stands.

North Ogden

Posted Oct. 22, 2026 · Updated Nov. 1, 2026 · Page look updated Nov. 2, 2026

Want every detail and source? Read the full case file

What is it?

A company wants to build up to 1,637 homes, condos and townhomes on about 500 acres of hillside below Ben Lomond Peak. That could bring about 5,000 new people.

Who wants to build it?

The land belongs to the Marriott family. Marriott Companies of Ogden is the developer. A company called Westside Investments LC asked the city to annex part of the land.

Was this land approved before?

Yes. In 2000 it was approved for 960 single-family homes. The new plan asks for more homes and adds condos, townhomes and a small shopping area.

Where does it stand?

  • Not approved yet. On Sept. 16 the Planning Commission put it on hold and asked for studies on traffic, water pressure and utilities.
  • No new vote is scheduled.
  • Annexation: 209 acres are in the county now. The developer wants North Ogden to take them in. The deadline to protest was Friday, Oct. 23, 2026, at 5 p.m. As of Nov. 1, there is no public record of a protest, and no city meeting has Altura on its agenda.
Only some people can file an annexation protest: certain landowners inside the area and some government boards. Simply disagreeing doesn't count. You can still speak at public meetings and contact the city.

What are neighbors worried about?

  • Enough water, and enough water pressure uphill
  • Traffic at 2600 North and Washington Boulevard
  • Flooding and drainage
  • Room in schools
  • Who pays for wider roads and new pipes

The developer says people "simply just don't want it in their backyard." More than 2,500 people signed a petition against the plan, KSL reported.

What can I do?

  • Watch the North Ogden public notices for the next meeting.
  • Ask the city to post the traffic and water studies.
  • See other projects on the developments map.
