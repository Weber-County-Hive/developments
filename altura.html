Altura: Up to 1,637 Proposed Housing Units on the North Ogden Bench

About 500 acres below Ben Lomond Peak are under city review. Part of the plan depends on North Ogden annexing 209 acres from the county. The protest deadline for that annexation is Friday, Oct. 23, 2026, at 5 p.m.

The short version

  • Altura proposes up to 1,637 housing units on the Ben Lomond foothills: 1,249 single-family homes and 388 condos or townhomes. That could add about 5,000 people, KSL reported.
  • In 2000 the same land was approved as North View Estates, a plan for 960 single-family homes.
  • About 291 acres are already in North Ogden. The developer wants the city to annex the other 209 acres from the county.
  • North Ogden's Planning Commission tabled the zoning and development agreement on Sept. 16 and asked for traffic, water-pressure and utility studies. No new vote has been scheduled.
  • Residents have raised water, traffic, drainage, school and cost concerns. A Change.org petition against the plan had more than 2,500 signatures, according to KSL.

What Altura is

Altura is a planned community on the north bench, at about 4000 North from 100 East to Nebo Avenue, below Ben Lomond Peak. Federal land borders it to the north and east, and the Bonneville Shoreline Trail crosses its northern edge.

Higher-density housing would sit near the village center, with larger estate lots at the edges.

Who's behind it

As the records name them:

  • Owner: the Marriott family (Building Salt Lake).
  • Developer: KSL names Marriott Companies of Ogden, with Amy Roskelley, its vice president of operations and development, speaking for it.
  • Annexation petitioner: Westside Investments LC, through Amy Roskelley (city notice).
  • At the Sept. 16 Planning Commission meeting: developer Brock Loomis presented, and designer Eric Langvart walked through the site plan (Citizen Portal).
  • Engineers: GRNOBL Civil Engineers worked on the plans with the city and the family (Building Salt Lake).
North Ogden's city attorney, Jon Call, told KSL: "It is not a city-pushed project."

Then vs. now

The land already has an approval from 2000. Today's plan asks for more housing units and different zoning.

The developer told KSL it already has a development agreement and the water rights, and that it could build on the 209 county acres under existing county zoning even without annexation. At the Sept. 16 meeting, resident Stephanie Goodrich asked to see the original 2000-era development agreement.

Where it stands

Nothing has been approved yet. The Planning Commission tabled Altura on Sept. 16 and hasn't taken it up again, and no City Council vote has been set.

  • Oct. 21, 2026The Planning Commission met. Altura was not on the agenda, though staff gave general project updates.
  • Oct. 14, 2026At the City Council, residents asked the city to post the full Altura documents and studies. No vote.
  • Oct. 7, 2026The Planning Commission meeting was canceled.
  • Sept. 23, 2026The annexation petition was certified.

  • Sept. 8, 2026The City Council voted 5–0 to accept the annexation petition for review, which "does not approve zoning or development."
  • Sept. 2, 2026The Planning Commission heard the developer's presentation.

Who can protest the annexation

Only an affected government entity's board, owners of rural real property inside the area, or owners of private property in a mining protection area can file a protest (Utah Code 10-2-810). General objections don't count as a protest.

Protests go to the Weber County Clerk/Auditor, 2380 Washington Blvd., Suite 320, Ogden, for the Boundary Commission, with a copy the same day to City Recorder Rian-Paige Strand, 505 E. 2600 North. Deadline: Oct. 23, 2026, 5 p.m.

What residents are asking

The main concerns are water, traffic and who pays for the roads and pipes.

  • Water: whether there is enough culinary and secondary water, and enough pressure uphill. At the Oct. 14 council meeting, residents described water-pressure problems.
  • Traffic: the plan feeds into 2600 North and Washington Boulevard, North Ogden's busiest intersection, and emergency access is limited. "Our main concern is traffic and it's water and it's the tax burden of who pays to widen the streets," resident Dave Schoellerman told KSL.
  • Drainage: neighbors reported past flooding and standing water on the bench.
  • Schools: whether schools have room.
  • Costs: who pays for road widening and other off-site upgrades.
  • Hillside: retaining-wall heights. A petition also cites avalanche risk and the city's hillside rules.
  • Phasing: some residents want building tied to infrastructure that's verified first.

The developer's side: Amy Roskelley told KSL, "I don't believe that they should be concerned. I think they simply just don't want it in their backyard." After meeting with developers, Stephanie Goodrich said: "No promises yet, but they were open." KSL reported the developers may revisit housing types.

Residents protested at 2600 North and Washington Boulevard on the first Saturday in October. A Change.org petition had more than 2,500 signatures by Oct. 6 (KSL). A separate iPetitions petition started by Travis Sanders had 473 when checked. Both ask the city to deny the rezone, master plan and development agreement as proposed.

What we don't know yet

  • What the original 2000 North View Estates development agreement allows, including on the county acres.
  • The exact unit count in the city's own staff report. KSL and a city update put it at 1,637; one petition says 1,650.
  • Whether the traffic, water-pressure and utility studies have been delivered, and whether the public can see them.
  • Whether any protests are filed by the Oct. 23 deadline, and when the City Council will hold its annexation hearing.
  • How Westside Investments LC, Marriott Companies and the Marriott family are connected.
  • The vote count on the Sept. 16 motion to table.
